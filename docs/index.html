Biological age clocks, locally referenced

An open registry where laboratories publish the clock they use and the local reference they built for it. Entries hold model parameters and aggregate performance only. There is no patient data here, and there never will be.

Research use only. Nothing on this site is a medical device or clinical advice. Entries are submitted by laboratories and are self-reported unless marked verified. A local score describes a position among local peers; it does not predict mortality or measure the pace of aging.

    03

    Calculator

    Computes biological age, the local standardized score and an approximate percentile using the parameters of the selected entry. Everything runs in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere.

    Groups come from the entry's empirical percentile cut points: P1 lowest 10%, P2 next 15%, P3 middle 50%, P4 next 15%, P5 highest 10%. The percentile is interpolated from those cut points, so it is approximate.

    04

    Add your laboratory

    No account on this site and no server: you contribute through a pull request on GitHub.

    1. Fork the repository and copy registry/template.json to registry/clocks/<your-id>.json.
    2. Fill in your clock parameters, local recentering coefficients, cut points and aggregate performance. A city and its centre (two decimals at most) are optional and put a pin on the map.
    3. Run python scripts/build_registry.py --check. It rejects anything that looks like patient-level data.
    4. Open a pull request. A check runs automatically; maintainers review it. New entries are shown as self-reported.

    Only aggregates and model parameters are accepted: no identifiers, no per-patient values, no per-patient scores.

    05

    About

    Routine blood tests already carry information about how a person is aging. A biological age clock turns a handful of those results, plus chronological age, into one number: the age at which an average person in a reference population would have the same profile. Most clocks were built in countries and laboratories far from where they end up being used, and a clock that runs well in one population can be systematically shifted in another.

    BioAge Local is a place for laboratories to share the clock they use and the local reference they built for it, so that a result is read against local peers instead of an imported average.

    How the reference clock is built

    TrainA PhenoAge-type Gompertz model is fitted against mortality in NHANES III with the BioAge R toolkit.
    EvaluateThe stored model is applied, unchanged, to NHANES IV and compared with chronological age.
    Apply locallyThe same model scores the laboratory's own routine results. No local survival data is needed.
    RecenterA local reference for age and sex turns each result into a standardized score and one of five groups, P1 to P5.

    The reference model, six-bioage, keeps six of the nine original PhenoAge markers: glucose, creatinine, lymphocyte percentage, MCV, RDW and white blood cell count. Albumin, C-reactive protein and alkaline phosphatase were dropped because of low local coverage. The survival parameters come from NHANES; the local step only defines a reference and does not recalibrate mortality risk.

    How to read a score

    Biological age minus chronological age is not interpreted on its own, because it shifts with age and sex in a local population. The score is the standardized residual of biological age after accounting for age and sex in the laboratory's own reference sample. A positive score means higher than local peers of the same age and sex, and the group (P1 lowest 10%, P3 the middle 50%, P5 highest 10%) says where that falls in the local distribution.

    What an entry contains

    • Model coefficients and the unit conversions of each marker
    • Local recentering coefficients and percentile cut points
    • Aggregate performance: n, correlation with age, RMSE, mean shift
    • Cohort summary: n, percentage female, mean age

    What it never contains

    • Individual results, identifiers or dates
    • Per-patient scores or raw columns of values
    • Laboratory addresses; a map pin marks a city or region only
    • Groups smaller than 100 people

    What this is not

    A local score is a position among local peers. It is not a diagnosis, a measure of the pace of aging or a mortality prediction for the individual. The link between the underlying model and mortality was established in NHANES, in another population, and has not been tested in local outcomes. Entries come from a single laboratory each and a referral sample is not the general population; some tests may be ordered for particular reasons, so who gets tested can shape the reference. Entries are self-reported until a maintainer marks them verified. The full pipeline, without any data, is in the repository so that other laboratories can reproduce the steps.

Biological age clocks, locally referenced

An open registry where laboratories publish the clock they use and the local reference they built for it. Entries hold model parameters and aggregate performance only. There is no patient data here, and there never will be.

Research use only. Nothing on this site is a medical device or clinical advice. Entries are submitted by laboratories and are self-reported unless marked verified. A local score describes a position among local peers; it does not predict mortality or measure the pace of aging.

    Calculator

    Computes biological age, the local standardized score and an approximate percentile using the parameters of the selected entry. Everything runs in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere.

    Groups come from the entry's empirical percentile cut points: P1 lowest 10%, P2 next 15%, P3 middle 50%, P4 next 15%, P5 highest 10%. The percentile is interpolated from those cut points, so it is approximate.

    Add your laboratory

    No account on this site and no server: you contribute through a pull request on GitHub.

    1. Fork the repository and copy registry/template.json to registry/clocks/<your-id>.json.
    2. Fill in your clock parameters, local recentering coefficients, cut points and aggregate performance. A city and its centre (two decimals at most) are optional and put a pin on the map.
    3. Run python scripts/build_registry.py --check. It rejects anything that looks like patient-level data.
    4. Open a pull request. A check runs automatically; maintainers review it. New entries are shown as self-reported.

    Only aggregates and model parameters are accepted: no identifiers, no per-patient values, no per-patient scores.

    About

    A biological age clock turns a few routine blood test results, plus chronological age, into one number: the age at which an average person in a reference population would have the same profile. Most clocks are built in other countries and other laboratories, and one that works well in a population can be shifted in another.

    This registry lets laboratories publish the clock they use and the local reference they built for it, so a result can be read against local peers instead of an imported average.

    The reference clock

    The clock six-bioage is a PhenoAge-type model with six of the nine original markers: glucose, creatinine, lymphocyte percentage, MCV, RDW and white blood cell count. Albumin, C-reactive protein and alkaline phosphatase were left out because of low local coverage. It was built in four steps:

    1. Fit a Gompertz model against mortality in NHANES III with the BioAge R toolkit.
    2. Apply the stored model, unchanged, to NHANES IV and compare it with chronological age.
    3. Apply the same model to the laboratory's routine results. No local survival data is needed.
    4. Build a local reference for age and sex, which turns each result into a standardized score and one of five groups, P1 to P5.

    The survival parameters come from NHANES. The local step only defines a reference; it does not recalibrate mortality risk.

    Reading a score

    Biological age minus chronological age is not read on its own, because it shifts with age and sex in a local population. The score is the standardized residual of biological age after accounting for age and sex in the laboratory's own reference sample. A positive score means higher than local peers of the same age and sex. The groups are P1 (lowest 10%), P2 (next 15%), P3 (middle 50%), P4 (next 15%) and P5 (highest 10%).

    What an entry contains

    Model coefficients and marker unit conversions, local recentering coefficients and percentile cut points, aggregate performance (n, correlation with age, RMSE, mean shift) and a cohort summary (n, percentage female, mean age). It never contains individual results, identifiers, dates, per-patient scores or laboratory addresses. A map pin marks a city or region only, and groups smaller than 100 people are rejected.

    Limits

    A local score is a position among local peers. It is not a diagnosis, a measure of the pace of aging or a mortality prediction for an individual. The link between the model and mortality was established in NHANES and has not been tested against local outcomes. Each entry comes from one laboratory, and a referral sample is not the general population: who gets tested can shape the reference. Entries are self-reported until a maintainer marks them verified. The pipeline, without data, is in the repository so other laboratories can reproduce the steps.
